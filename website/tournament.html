HOW CLOSE, AT WHAT COST?

Probability to reallocate.

Add the absolute target–simulation differences and divide by two. That’s total variation distance (TV). We display 100 × TV in percentage points: lower means closer to our chosen benchmark.

A score of 12 points means reallocating 12 percentage points of title probability would match the target. Excess and deficit are the same probability moving between teams, so we count it once.

Small estimated TV differences are not decisive. Sampling makes TV positive even under perfect alignment. More simulations can refine estimates; they cannot make this fairness preference an objective truth. Average champion rating gap is secondary: smaller gaps mean stronger champions, not necessarily fairer title shares.

A sampling-noise reference, not a confidence interval for TV

The notebook draws 2,000 multinomial batches directly from the target at the same sample size. Their TV range illustrates sampling noise under perfect alignment. It is not a format’s TV confidence interval, a significance test, or a reason to subtract a “noise correction” from the score.

THE OBJECTIVE CHANGES THE ANSWER

Strong champions.
Fair chances.
Different questions.

A long league can be good at selecting a strong champion while concentrating title chances more than our benchmark allows. That isn’t a contradiction: champion strength and the distribution of chances measure different things.

Our benchmark gives stronger teams more weight without giving them the trophy by default. Another definition of fairness could prefer equal chances, stronger seeding, or always selecting the top-rated team. The choice should stay visible.

FOR THE CURIOUS

Under the hood

The target and match modelweight_i = 10 ** ((rating_i − max_rating) / 600)
target_share_i = weight_i / sum(weights)
TV = 0.5 × sum(abs(simulated_share_i − target_share_i))

The match model is P(A wins) = 1 / (1 + 10 ** (−(rating_A − rating_B) / 600)). Equivalently, P(A wins) = weight_A / (weight_A + weight_B). Normalizing these weights into title shares is an additional fairness preference; the pairwise model does not force a tournament to produce those shares.

Fixed FIFA ratings for 32 men’s national teams, independent W/L outcomes conditional on ratings, neutral venues. Every match has a winner. No draws, fatigue, home advantage or rating updates. This borrows FIFA’s expected-result curve as a simplified W/L model, not an official or calibrated win forecast.

Exact format rules and demo scope
  • Knockout: random permutation into a fixed bracket or full recursive rating seeding: 1–32, 2–31, etc. Top seeds kept apart; seeds 1 and 2 can meet only in the final. 31 matches.
  • World Cup: four rating pots of eight, one random team per pot in each group. Eight single-round-robin groups; top two advance. Knockout: A1–B2, C1–D2, E1–F2, G1–H2 | B1–A2, D1–C2, F1–E2, H1–G2. No geographic restrictions or third-place match. 63 matches.
  • League: double round robin; most wins is champion. 992 matches.
  • League + best-of-seven: top 16 from the league, seeded by its standings in a fixed bracket; no reseeding. Each series ends at four wins. Total theoretical cost 1,052–1,097 matches.
  • Swiss: a full pairing each round minimizes total absolute current-win differences, with random tie weights and no rematches. No byes or backtracking; infeasible full pairings raise an error. Everyone plays once per round. The demo offers 1–15 rounds; saved analysis exists only for 5, 7 and 9.

All standings ties are random lots, never rating. Swiss is an educational variant, not official federation rules. The browser uses a locally bundled Blossom matching implementation to preserve the optimization rule. Browser randomness is fresh and does not reproduce NumPy’s seed. Replay retains the same demo history.

Batch, uncertainty and match budgets

The saved batch uses 1,000 tournaments per setting, seed 42. League and league-plus-playoffs reuse the same season within each simulation. The revised objective re-analyzes the existing batch; it does not fabricate new simulations.

Title counts divided by repetitions estimate championship shares. Signed differences are observed minus target, in percentage points. Individual Wilson intervals are not simultaneous intervals for all teams. No confidence interval for a format’s TV is claimed.

Hard budgets must use max_possible_matches, especially the 1,097-match playoff maximum—not the observed average or observed maximum. We show all tested costs, without recommending untested formats or interpolating uncomputed Swiss settings.

Secondary champion rating gap = highest input rating − champion’s input rating. It measures champion strength; it is no longer the main success score.
